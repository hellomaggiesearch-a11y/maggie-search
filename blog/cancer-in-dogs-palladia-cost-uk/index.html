Cancer in Dogs: What Palladia (Toceranib) Really Costs in the UK (And How to Pay Less)

Hearing "it's cancer" is hard enough. Then comes the treatment quote and the worry doubles. If your vet has mentioned Palladia, here is what it is, whether it works, what it really costs in the UK — and how to pay less without cutting your dog's care.

Maggie Search Editorial Team · Published 12 September 2026 · Last updated 12 September 2026

Mast cell tumours are one of the most common skin cancers in dogs. When the tumour cannot be fully removed by surgery, comes back, or has already spread, a veterinary oncologist may recommend Palladia — the first oral chemotherapy drug licensed specifically for dogs.

It works, but it is expensive — and because it is an ongoing medication, the cost builds month after month. The good news: you can treat well for less than many owners expect. Let's look at the real numbers.

⚠️ Important note

What Palladia (Toceranib) Actually Is

Palladia is the brand name for toceranib phosphate, made by Zoetis. It is a tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI) — a targeted therapy that works in two ways at once:

  • It attacks the tumour cell directly, blocking the KIT receptor, which is faulty in many mast cell tumours.
  • It cuts off the tumour's blood supply, inhibiting the VEGFR2 and PDGFRβ receptors that cancers use to grow their own vessels.

The licensed indication is for non-resectable, recurrent, cutaneous Patnaik grade II or III mast cell tumours. Off-licence, oncologists also use toceranib for other tumours (such as anal sac and some carcinomas), always under specialist supervision.

Does It Actually Work? What the Research Says

In the pivotal trial behind the drug's licence (London et al., Clinical Cancer Research, 2009), among 145 dogs treated with toceranib the objective response rate was 42.8% — the tumour shrank or disappeared. In the placebo-controlled phase, the response was 37.2% with Palladia versus 7.9% with placebo.

So it is not a guarantee for every dog, but for a meaningful share the benefit is real. Response and tolerance vary from dog to dog — only your oncologist, with the right tests, can say whether it is worth it in your case.

What It Really Costs in the UK

Palladia comes as 10 mg, 15 mg and 50 mg tablets, so the dose can be matched to your dog's weight. As a guide, typical UK per-tablet prices are around:

The licensed dose is roughly 3.25 mg/kg every other day (many oncologists reduce this to about 2.4–2.75 mg/kg, or a Monday/Wednesday/Friday schedule, to improve tolerance). In practice, for a medium-sized dog the cost of the tablets alone often lands between £250 and £450 a month — before check-ups and blood tests. Figures are reference estimates to help you plan, not a quote; always check the current price before you buy.

Why the Active Ingredient Rule Works Differently Here

On most medicines, the biggest saving comes from choosing the same active ingredient in a cheaper form. Palladia is the exception: toceranib is patented and single-source (Zoetis), with no generic. So you cannot switch to a cheaper version of the same molecule — the saving has to come from where you buy it and from which treatment plan your oncologist chooses. There are clear structural reasons why the same pet medicine costs different amounts across UK pharmacies, and they apply here too.

Side Effects and Monitoring

As a chemotherapy drug, Palladia needs monitoring. The most common side effects are diarrhoea, reduced appetite, weight loss and blood in the stool. It can also cause a drop in white blood cells (neutropenia), high blood pressure and protein loss in the urine. Your oncologist will run regular blood, kidney, blood-pressure and urine checks and may pause or adjust the dose — which can also help manage the cost.

How to Pay Less — Without Compromising Care

1. Ask your vet for a written prescription

In the UK you are legally entitled to a written prescription (usually for a small fee). You can then buy the same Palladia from a VMD-registered online pharmacy, which is often much cheaper than the practice dispensary. For an ongoing treatment, this one request is usually the single largest saving available.

2. Compare suppliers before every refill

Because there is no generic, the saving comes from where you buy. The identical tablets can vary a lot between pharmacies — comparing your exact strength before each refill turns a monthly shock into a managed budget.

3. Ask your oncologist about alternatives

In some cases there is masitinib (Masivet), another tyrosine kinase inhibitor, or metronomic chemotherapy — low doses of cheaper drugs such as cyclophosphamide with an NSAID. It is always a clinical decision, but "Is there an equally suitable option that might cost less?" is a fair question.

4. Check charity help if money is tight

The PDSA, Blue Cross and the RSPCA offer support or lower-cost treatment for eligible owners. It is worth weighing the honest pros and cons of pet insurance too — a lifetime policy taken out before diagnosis makes ongoing treatment far easier to sustain.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there a generic version of Palladia in the UK?

No. Toceranib is single-source (Zoetis) with no generic. The saving comes from comparing suppliers for the same product and from discussing alternatives — such as masitinib or metronomic chemotherapy — with your oncologist.

How much does Palladia cost in the UK?

As a guide, around £5.38 (10 mg), £7.13 (15 mg) and £19.34 (50 mg) per tablet. For a medium-sized dog the tablets alone often come to roughly £250–£450 a month, before consultations and blood tests.

Can I buy Palladia without a prescription in the UK?

No. Toceranib is a POM-V medicine and legally requires a veterinary prescription. You can ask for a written prescription and buy from any VMD-registered pharmacy.

Does Palladia actually work?

In the pivotal trial the objective response rate was 42.8% among 145 dogs, and 37.2% versus 7.9% for placebo in the controlled phase. It is not a guarantee, but for a meaningful share the benefit is real.

Can I use human cancer drugs to save money?

Not on your own. Some human drugs are used in specific veterinary situations, but that is strictly your vet's decision — self-medicating can be dangerous.

What To Do This Week

  • Ask your vet for a written prescription so you are free to compare prices.
  • Write down the active ingredient and dose — "toceranib 50 mg every other day" — not just the brand.
  • Ask your oncologist about alternatives such as masitinib or metronomic chemotherapy.
  • Compare the price of your exact tablet strength across UK pharmacies before your next refill.
  • Budget for the monitoring tests — blood count, kidney function, blood pressure and urine — as part of the treatment.
  • Check charity or insurance options if the monthly cost is a strain.

The Bottom Line

A cancer diagnosis sounds frightening, but for many dogs Palladia buys real, good-quality time. What makes it sustainable is paying less for the same medicine — comparing suppliers, using a written prescription, and asking your oncologist the right questions — so the treatment fits your budget every month.
