Apoquel for Dogs: What It Really Costs in the UK (And How to Pay Less)

If your vet has just prescribed Apoquel for your dog's allergic itching, you've probably noticed two things already: how fast it seems to work — and how quickly the cost of Apoquel adds up in the UK once you realise it's a tablet for life, not a one-off course.

Maggie Search Editorial Team · Published 3 September 2026 · Last updated 3 September 2026

The scratching that starts at the door and doesn't stop by bedtime, the raw patches behind the ears, the sleepless nights for both of you — allergic and atopic dermatitis in dogs is relentless, and it's usually chronic. When the vet reaches for Apoquel (oclacitinib), it's often because owners want relief that starts working now, not in six weeks.

Here's what oclacitinib for dogs really costs in the UK, why it's priced the way it is, and the legitimate ways to bring the bill down without cutting corners on your dog's care.

Fast, persistent scratching is often the first sign vets treat with Apoquel.

What Is Apoquel, and Why Is It Different?

Apoquel (oclacitinib, made by Zoetis) is a Janus kinase (JAK) inhibitor licensed in the UK to control the itch of allergic dermatitis and the clinical signs of atopic dermatitis in dogs aged 12 months and over. Rather than damping down the whole immune system, it blocks specific JAK enzyme signals involved in the itch-and-inflammation pathway — which is part of why it tends to act so fast.

Its real edge is speed. Many dogs show noticeably less itching within 4 to 24 hours of the first dose — a stark contrast to slower-acting immunomodulators such as ciclosporin, where Atopica typically takes four to six weeks to show an effect. That speed makes Apoquel a popular first choice for a dog mid-flare, though it comes with a different long-term cost profile to weigh up with your vet.

Dosing starts at twice daily for up to 14 days, then usually drops to a once-daily maintenance dose. Tablets come in three strengths — 3.6 mg, 5.4 mg and 16 mg — dosed by your dog's bodyweight, and a flavoured Apoquel Chewable (same active ingredient) is also available for dogs who won't take a plain tablet.

What Does Apoquel Actually Cost in the UK?

Like most allergy medicines, there's no single UK price — and that's exactly what catches owners out. Apoquel is a lifelong, weight-based daily tablet: a bigger dog needs a bigger, pricier daily dose, and unlike some other veterinary medicines, oclacitinib currently has no generic equivalent on the UK market, so the branded product is what you'll be quoted. Multiply the daily dose across every month your dog needs it, and the running total — not the price of one box — is the number that actually matters for your budget.

Why the Same Tablet Can Cost Very Different Amounts

Three factors explain most of the difference between what one owner pays and what another does:

  • Your dog's weight. Apoquel is dosed by bodyweight and tablet strength, so a large breed needs a materially bigger — and pricier — daily dose than a small one.
  • Where you buy it. The CMA's investigation into the UK veterinary market found that prices for the same product vary significantly between practices, and buying directly from a practice is usually dearer than a licensed online pharmacy dispensing against a written prescription.
  • How the dose is managed over time. A vet who reviews and, where appropriate, tapers the maintenance dose can bring the monthly cost down without losing control of the itch.

4 Legitimate Ways to Pay Less for Apoquel

1. Ask your vet for a written prescription. Under RCVS rules, a vet who is already prescribing a medicine will provide a written prescription for it (a small fee may apply). With that prescription, a licensed UK online pharmacy can often dispense the same Apoquel for meaningfully less than practice prices — a typical 40–70% saving on repeat medication bought this way.

2. Compare prices between practices, not just online. Because Apoquel has no generic to switch to, comparing where you buy it matters even more than usual. The CMA's vet-market investigation found genuine price variation for the same product between practices — so it's worth checking what your dog's exact strength and pack size costs elsewhere before assuming your current price is standard.

3. Ask about the lowest effective maintenance dose. Once the itch is under control, some dogs can be managed on a lower or less frequent dose than the one used to bring the initial flare-up down. Fewer or smaller tablets per month means a smaller bill — always a conversation for your review appointment, never something to adjust yourself.

4. Check your insurance timing. Allergic and atopic dermatitis are chronic conditions, so when your policy started matters. If the itching was noted before cover began, it's likely to be excluded as pre-existing. A lifetime policy with a healthy annual limit is what keeps ongoing allergy treatment covered year after year — worth reading up on whether pet insurance is worth it for an older dog before any skin condition appears.

Apoquel Alternatives Worth Discussing With Your Vet

Apoquel isn't the only option, and it isn't right for every dog:

  • Atopica / ciclosporin: a slower-acting immunomodulator, usually taking four to six weeks to work, but a well-established long-term option — see our full breakdown of what Atopica costs in the UK.
  • Cytopoint (lokivetmab): a monthly injection given at the practice, dosed by weight, that targets the itch signal directly rather than being taken daily at home.
  • Steroids: fast and inexpensive for short-term flare-ups, but generally unsuitable for continuous long-term use.
  • Allergen-specific immunotherapy: a longer-term "allergy vaccine" approach that can reduce reliance on daily medication in suitable dogs.

The right choice depends on your dog's age, other health conditions and how they respond to treatment — a conversation for your vet, not a price comparison site. What is our job: making sure you never overpay for whichever option you choose.

The Bottom Line

Apoquel earns its place for dogs who need itch relief fast — but "fast-acting" doesn't mean "one-off": for most dogs it's a lifelong, weight-based daily cost. There's no generic to switch to, so the two real levers are where you buy it and how tightly the maintenance dose is managed. A written prescription, honest comparison between practices, and a regular conversation with your vet about the lowest effective dose can meaningfully cut the lifetime cost of treatment without compromising your dog's comfort. If speed of relief isn't the priority for your dog, it's also worth weighing Apoquel against the lifelong cost of Atopica before committing to either.
