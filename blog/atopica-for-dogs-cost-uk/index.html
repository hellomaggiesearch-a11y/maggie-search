Atopica for Dogs: What Cyclosporine Really Costs in the UK (And How to Pay Less)

If your vet has prescribed Atopica for your dog's relentless itching, you've probably had two reactions: relief that something might finally calm the scratching — and a quiet gasp when you saw the price of a lifelong daily capsule.

Maggie Search Editorial Team · Published 3 September 2026 · Last updated 3 September 2026

The constant licking of a paw, the thumping of a back leg scratching at 3am, the sore patches that never quite heal — canine atopic dermatitis is exhausting for the dog and the owner alike. And when the vet reaches for Atopica, it's often one of the more expensive long-term skin treatments you'll be quoted.

Here's what cyclosporine really costs in the UK, why the same treatment can cost very different amounts, and the legitimate ways to bring the bill down without cutting your dog's care.

What Is Atopica, and Why Is It Different?

Atopica (cyclosporine, also spelled ciclosporin) is an immunomodulator licensed in the UK for canine atopic dermatitis — the chronic, allergic skin disease behind so much lifelong itching. Unlike steroids or anti-inflammatories, it doesn't simply mask the itch: it works on the immune system, which is where the allergy actually starts.

In plain English: cyclosporine calms the overactive immune cells (T-lymphocytes) that drive the allergic reaction in the skin. For many dogs it becomes a steroid-sparing option — a way to control the allergy long-term without the downsides of continuous steroid use. Older dogs often carry more than one lifelong prescription at once; the same "cost of a daily tablet, forever" maths applies to conditions like hypothyroidism in dogs and Cushing's disease.

But there's a catch that matters for your wallet: cyclosporine works gradually. Improvement usually takes four to six weeks, so it's a long-term control treatment — not a quick fix for a sudden flare-up.

What Does Atopica Actually Cost in the UK?

There's no single price, and that's exactly the problem. Atopica is a daily, weight-based medication, sold in different strengths — typically 10 mg, 25 mg, 50 mg and 100 mg capsules — so a Labrador needs a far bigger (and pricier) daily dose than a Yorkshire Terrier. Multiply that daily dose across every month of the year, and the "lifetime cost" is the number that really matters.

Cyclosporine is prescription-only and needs ongoing veterinary oversight.

Why the Same Treatment Can Cost Very Different Amounts

Three factors drive the difference:

  • Your dog's weight. Dosing is by bodyweight, so bigger dogs need bigger, more expensive daily doses.
  • Brand versus generic. The same active ingredient, cyclosporine, is sold as branded Atopica and as licensed UK generics like Cyclavance and Sporimune — often at very different prices for an identical molecule.
  • Where you buy it. The CMA's investigation into the UK vet market found that prices vary significantly between practices, and buying from a practice is usually dearer than a licensed online pharmacy dispensing against a written prescription.

4 Legitimate Ways to Pay Less for Atopica

1. Ask your vet for a written prescription. Under the RCVS code, a vet will provide a written prescription for a medicine they're already prescribing (they may charge a small fee for it). With that prescription, a licensed UK online pharmacy can often dispense the same cyclosporine for far less than practice prices — the standard 40–70% online saving on repeat medication.

2. Compare by the active ingredient, not the brand. This is the lever most owners miss. Branded Atopica and generics like Cyclavance and Sporimune all contain the same active ingredient. Because formulation affects how well cyclosporine is absorbed, the switch itself should always be your vet's call — but knowing the equivalents exist is what stops you overpaying for the brand name alone.

3. Ask whether the dose can be tapered. Once the allergy is under control, vets commonly reduce cyclosporine to the lowest effective dose — often every other day, or just twice a week. Fewer capsules a month means a smaller bill, so it's worth asking about a step-down plan at your review appointments.

4. Check your insurance timing. Atopic dermatitis is a lifelong condition, so when your policy started matters enormously. If the itching was noted before you took out cover, it's likely excluded as pre-existing. A lifetime policy with a high annual limit is what keeps a chronic skin treatment covered year after year — it's worth weighing whether pet insurance is worth it for an older dog before any condition appears.

Atopica Alternatives Worth Discussing With Your Vet

Cyclosporine isn't the only option, and it isn't right for every dog:

  • Apoquel (oclacitinib): a daily tablet that works far faster on itching, often within a day — but with a different long-term profile to weigh up.
  • Cytopoint (lokivetmab): a monthly injection that targets the itch signal directly, dosed by weight.
  • Steroids: cheap and fast for short-term flare-ups, but not ideal for continuous long-term use.
  • Allergen-specific immunotherapy: a longer-term "allergy vaccine" approach for suitable dogs.

The right choice depends on your dog's age, other conditions and how they respond — a conversation for your vet, not a price comparison site. What is our job: making sure you never overpay for whichever option you choose.

The Bottom Line

Atopica genuinely works — but the price you pay for it is far more negotiable than most owners realise. The active ingredient is identical whether it's branded or generic; the bill isn't. A written prescription, a comparison by active ingredient, and an honest chat with your vet about tapering the dose can cut the lifetime cost of treatment dramatically, without compromising your dog's care.
