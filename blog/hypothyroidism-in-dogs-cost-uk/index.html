Hypothyroidism in Dogs: What the Medication Really Costs in the UK (And How to Pay Less)

Your dog has slowed down. Putting on weight despite the same meals, sleeping more, the coat looking thin and lifeless. It is easy to file all of it under "getting older" — but this exact picture is the classic sign of an underactive thyroid, one of the most common hormonal problems in dogs.

Maggie Search Editorial Team · Published 31 August 2026 · Last updated 31 August 2026

Here is the good news first: hypothyroidism is one of the most rewarding chronic conditions to treat. With the right daily hormone — levothyroxine — most dogs go back to being themselves. The catch nobody spells out at that first appointment: the treatment is for life, and the price of the very same medicine varies widely between brands and pharmacies.

This guide covers what hypothyroidism actually is, the signs worth acting on, how it is diagnosed, and — the part most owners are never shown — why the same prescription can cost very different amounts across UK pharmacies, and how to pay less without ever compromising your dog's care.

What Hypothyroidism in Dogs Actually Is

The thyroid is a gland in the neck that produces hormones — mainly T4 — which set the body's metabolic rate, the speed at which everything runs. In hypothyroidism the gland produces too little, and the whole body slows down.

It typically appears in middle age (around four to ten years) and is most common in medium and large breeds — Golden Retriever, Labrador, Boxer, Dobermann, Setter, Cocker Spaniel and others. In most cases the cause is the gradual destruction of the gland (autoimmune thyroiditis or atrophy).

Don't confuse it with hyperthyroidism

In dogs the problem is almost always too little hormone (hypothyroidism). Hyperthyroidism — too much — is rare in dogs and common in older cats. They are opposite conditions with opposite treatments.

The Signs Worth Acting On

Look at the cluster, not a single symptom. The signs come on slowly and are easily read as simple ageing:

  • Weight gain without eating any more
  • Lethargy and low mood — sleeping more, playing less, tiring easily
  • Seeking warmth — an apparent intolerance of the cold
  • A dull coat and hair loss, often symmetrical on the flanks, with the classic "rat tail" (a thinning, bald tail)
  • Darkened, thickened or greasy skin, with recurrent skin and ear infections
  • Slow healing and hair that does not grow back after clipping
  • In advanced cases, a "tragic" facial expression (myxoedema) and a slower heart rate

If several of these ring true, it is worth investigating rather than writing it off as age.

How It's Diagnosed

You cannot diagnose it from the signs alone — several conditions look similar. Your vet confirms it with blood tests, read together as a picture:

  • Total T4 and free T4 — the thyroid hormones
  • Canine TSH — the hormone that tells the thyroid to work

There is an important trap here: other illnesses — and even stress or certain medicines — can lower T4 without true hypothyroidism (so-called "sick euthyroid syndrome"). That is why the results are always interpreted by the vet alongside the clinical picture, and why a single low reading is not a diagnosis on its own.

The Treatment: Levothyroxine for Life

The treatment is simple and effective: replace the missing hormone with levothyroxine sodium, a synthetic T4 identical to the natural one. There is no cure, but there is complete control — usually one or two doses a day, for good.

One point that surprises many owners: dogs need far higher doses than people do (relative to body weight). So your dog's dose is calculated by the vet — never copy a human dose.

The same active ingredient, different labels

In the UK, levothyroxine for dogs is available as VMD-authorised veterinary products under more than one brand. Knowing the active ingredient, not just the brand name, is the single most useful thing you can learn — because the same active ingredient can appear at very different prices.

The Number Nobody Shows You

A tablet of levothyroxine is cheap on its own. The weight is in the sum: every day, for years, plus the blood tests that check the dose. And the same active ingredient can cost noticeably more from one pharmacy than another.

Here is where some owners make the most dangerous decision available to them: they stretch the tablets to make a pack last, or skip days, or simply buy whatever came up first. Missing doses does not save money — it undoes the very thing you are paying for. The answer is not less medicine; it is paying less for the same medicine. There are clear structural reasons why the same pet medicine costs different amounts across UK pharmacies.

Ways to Pay Less — Without Compromising Care

1. Ask your vet for a written prescription

In the UK you are legally entitled to ask your vet for a written prescription and buy the medication from a VMD-registered pharmacy of your choice. A vet cannot refuse to write one for a medicine they are already prescribing to your animal. For a lifelong daily treatment, this one request is the largest single saving available to most owners.

2. Ask about the same active ingredient under a different brand

Because levothyroxine is available from more than one manufacturer, there may be an equally suitable, VMD-authorised product with the same active ingredient at a lower price. Only your vet can decide what is appropriate, and a UK prescription is written for a specific product — but you are entitled to the conversation.

3. Sort out insurance before a diagnosis, not after

Once hypothyroidism is on the record it becomes a pre-existing condition, and cover for it becomes very hard to arrange. If you have a younger dog, this matters now rather than later. It is worth weighing the honest pros and cons of pet insurance for older pets while you still have the choice.

Getting the Most From the Treatment

Levothyroxine works very well, as long as it is given consistently. A few things make the difference:

  • Keep timing and food consistent. Food affects absorption, so give it at the same time and in the same relation to meals each day.
  • Recheck by blood test. Your vet will repeat the T4 around four to eight weeks after starting or changing the dose, usually measured a few hours after the tablet.
  • Don't switch brand or product on your own. Not all products are absorbed identically; if you change, the vet will normally recheck the level.
  • Be patient. Energy tends to return within a few weeks; the coat can take a few months to fully recover.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can hypothyroidism in dogs be cured?

No — but it is one of the most controllable chronic conditions in dogs. Levothyroxine replaces the hormone the thyroid no longer makes, and with the right dose and monitoring most dogs return to normal. Treatment continues for life.

How much does levothyroxine for dogs cost in the UK?

It depends on your dog's size, the product prescribed and where you buy. What is consistent is the spread: the same active ingredient can differ substantially in price between pharmacies, and originator brands usually cost more than VMD-authorised alternatives.

What are the first signs of hypothyroidism in a dog?

Usually a cluster rather than one sign: weight gain without eating more, low energy, seeking warmth, and a dull coat with symmetrical hair loss. They come on slowly and are easily mistaken for ageing, which is why a blood test matters.

Can a dog with hypothyroidism live a normal life?

Yes. With the correct diagnosis, daily levothyroxine and periodic blood tests, the large majority of dogs regain their energy, weight and coat and live entirely normal lives.

What To Do This Week

  • Book a blood test if your middle-aged dog is gaining weight, slowing down or losing coat.
  • Ask your vet for a written prescription so you are free to compare prices.
  • Write down the active ingredient (levothyroxine sodium) and the exact dose — your key to finding equivalents.
  • Compare the price of your dog's exact product across UK pharmacies before your next refill.
  • Keep to the recheck your vet books after starting treatment.
  • Review insurance — especially for any other pets not yet diagnosed.

⚠️ Never change thyroid medication without your vet

Never start, stop, space out or substitute your dog's levothyroxine without your vet's explicit guidance, and never copy a human dose. The dose is finely matched to your dog and confirmed by blood tests. This article helps you pay less for the treatment your vet has prescribed — not replace their advice.
