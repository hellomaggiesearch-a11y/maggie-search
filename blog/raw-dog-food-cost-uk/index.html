The first time you open a bag of kibble and read the ingredients properly, it's hard not to wonder whether there's something better. Raw feeding — complete meals of raw meat, offal, bone and vegetables, delivered frozen — promises exactly that, and a lot of UK owners swear by it: shinier coats, smaller poos, a dog who actually gets excited at dinner time.

What nobody puts on the box is the honest monthly cost of raw dog food in the UK, or the fact that raw isn't safe for every dog or every household. This guide does both: real cost-per-day maths by dog size, what subscription brands actually charge, and a clear list of when raw is a conversation for your vet first.

⚠️ Don't switch to raw without your vet if…

  • Your dog is on immune-suppressing treatment — for example ciclosporin (Atopica), long-term steroids, or chemotherapy such as Palladia. Raw meat can carry bacteria such as Salmonella, and these dogs are less able to fight it off.
  • Your dog has kidney disease, a history of pancreatitis, or is on a prescription diet — their food is part of their treatment.
  • Someone at home is very young, elderly, pregnant or immunocompromised. The British Small Animal Veterinary Association (BSAVA) advises these households to get medical advice before preparing or storing raw pet food.

How Much Raw Food Does a Dog Eat a Day?

Raw feeding is portioned by body weight. A common starting point for an adult dog is 2–3% of its ideal body weight per day, then adjusted to keep your dog in good shape. Small and very active dogs tend to need the higher end; couch-loving or overweight dogs the lower end. Puppies need a lot more.

At a middle-of-the-road 2.5%, that works out as:

  • 5 kg dog (e.g. a Shih Tzu): about 125 g a day
  • 10 kg dog (e.g. a Beagle): about 250 g a day
  • 20 kg dog (e.g. a Springer Spaniel): about 500 g a day
  • 30 kg dog (e.g. a Labrador): about 750 g a day

Every brand publishes its own feeding guide or calculator, and that should be your actual number — but this gives you a realistic ballpark before you start looking at prices.

Is Raw More Expensive Than Kibble?

Almost always, yes. A good-quality dry food for a medium dog typically costs noticeably less per day than a complete raw diet, and the gap widens the bigger the dog. Some owners close it by feeding raw as a topper — a portion of raw mixed into a complete dry or wet food — which gives the dog some of what they love about raw at a fraction of the full cost.

What raw doesn't have is strong scientific evidence that it's healthier than a good complete commercial diet. Plenty of owners see real changes in their dog; vets' professional bodies remain cautious, mainly because of bacterial risk and poorly balanced home-made diets. If you go raw, choosing a recipe labelled complete (not "complementary") is the single most important thing — it means it's designed to be fed on its own.

Raw Food and the Itchy Dog

A lot of owners look at raw food because their dog is scratching. It's worth being honest here: raw food is not automatically hypoallergenic. Most food allergies in dogs are reactions to a specific protein — often a very common one, such as chicken or beef. If your dog reacts to chicken, a chicken-based raw meal won't help, however natural it is.

The recognised way to find out whether food is behind the itch is an elimination diet trial: around eight weeks on a single diet your vet chooses — usually a hydrolysed or single novel-protein food — with nothing else, not even treats or flavoured chews. In dogs that really do have a food allergy, a properly followed trial gets a response in the vast majority of cases.

Raw Feeding Safely at Home

If you and your vet are happy with raw, the BSAVA's message is simple: treat it like raw meat for people.

  • Defrost in the fridge, covered, on the bottom shelf — never on the worktop.
  • Keep separate boards, bowls and utensils, and clean them with hot soapy water after every meal.
  • Wash your hands after handling the food, the bowl, and after picking up after your dog.
  • Don't leave uneaten raw food down — lift it after 20–30 minutes.
  • Be extra careful about face-licking if there are babies or vulnerable people in the house.
