Pet Insurance: The Honest Pros and Cons (and Why a Plan Almost Always Wins for Older Pets)

Let's Be Honest First: the Cons of a Pet Plan

A good article about pet insurance doesn't pretend it's flawless. It isn't. Here's the fair, straight version of the drawbacks — the same points a decent, honest advisor would tell you before you buy.

The honest downsides

  • You pay every month, even in the healthy years. If your pet never gets ill, those premiums don't come back — you're buying cover, not a savings account.
  • Premiums rise as your pet ages. Older pets claim more, so lifetime cover costs more over time.
  • Pre-existing conditions usually aren't covered. Which is exactly why when you start matters.
  • There's an excess, and sometimes a co-payment. It's rarely "£0 out of pocket".
  • Waiting periods apply. Illness cover usually starts after a short window, not the instant you sign up.

That's the honest list. Now here's the part that changes everything: none of those cons come close to the cost of one serious vet bill without any cover at all.

A pet plan turns a terrifying four-figure bill into a manageable, predictable monthly cost. That's the whole point.

2. It lets you decide with your heart, not your bank balance

This is the one most owners don't think about until it's too late. Without cover, treatment becomes a maths problem. With a plan, when the vet says "we can treat this," your answer is simply "yes, please." No agonising. No "economic euthanasia". No guilt. That peace of mind is the real product you're buying.

3. Lifetime cover keeps paying, year after year

The best plans are lifetime policies: conditions diagnosed while you're covered stay covered at each renewal, up to that year's limit. For chronic, ongoing illnesses — arthritis, diabetes, heart disease — where costs repeat for years, this is where insurance quietly saves owners thousands.

4. It often includes more than you'd expect

Strong modern policies bundle in extras a savings pot never could — behavioural treatment, complementary therapies like hydrotherapy and osteopathy, dental cover, and 24/7 access to qualified vets by video call. More value, fewer excluded surprises.

Why Older Pets Change the Maths Completely

If your dog or cat is entering their senior years, this section is written for you.

Photo: Unsplash

Ageing pets simply need the vet more — and for bigger things. Chronic conditions like arthritis, kidney disease, heart disease and cancer become far more common with age, and they're exactly the expensive, long-running problems that cover is built for. The maths that's merely sensible for a young pet becomes compelling for an older one.

There's a catch owners hit here: traditionally, many insurers made cover for senior pets hard to get, or priced it out of reach. That's changing. Everypaw recently launched pet insurance designed specifically for older cats, dogs and rabbits — because a pet shouldn't lose protection just for reaching their golden years.

The one honest caveat

The best time to insure is before a problem appears. Once a condition is diagnosed, it's usually classed as pre-existing. So if your pet is getting older, waiting is the one move that genuinely costs you.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is pet insurance worth it in the UK?

For most owners, yes. A single serious claim often runs £1,000–£3,000 or more, which usually outweighs years of premiums — and cover means treatment decisions aren't limited by cost. If your pet never claims, you've paid for protection you were glad not to need.

Can I insure an older pet?

Yes. Some insurers, including Everypaw, offer cover designed for older cats, dogs and rabbits. The key is to start before a condition appears, because pre-existing conditions are usually excluded.

What isn't covered by pet insurance?

Typically pre-existing conditions, routine or preventative care unless you add a wellness option, and anything within the policy's waiting period. Always check the terms and the excess before you buy.

The Bottom Line

Here's the truth, and then you decide. Yes, a pet plan has real costs and real limits. You pay monthly, premiums rise with age, and pre-existing conditions sit outside cover. We haven't hidden any of that.

But weigh it against a single £2,800 surgery, a £1,254 cancer claim, or years of diabetes care — and especially if your pet is getting older — and the conclusion is hard to argue with: a plan almost always wins. Not because someone's selling it to you, but because the numbers, and the peace of mind, land firmly on the side of the owner who's covered. The one thing that only gets more expensive is waiting.
