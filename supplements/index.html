Why compare supplements by ingredient, not by brand

Pet supplements are one of the fastest-growing corners of UK pet care — the nutraceuticals market is worth hundreds of millions of pounds and is growing at roughly 10% a year. That growth has a side effect: dozens of tubs that look different, are priced very differently, and yet often lean on the same handful of active ingredients. Glucosamine, chondroitin, green-lipped mussel, omega-3 (EPA and DHA), probiotics and L-tryptophan turn up again and again under different names.

Maggie Search treats supplements the same way it treats medicines: we group them by their active ingredient so you can compare like with like. A joint chew and a joint powder that both rely on glucosamine and green-lipped mussel are, nutritionally, closer than their packaging suggests — and the price per day can differ enormously. Our job is to make that visible. We are not a shop and we do not ship anything: when you pick a product, you go straight to a registered UK pharmacy, and they handle payment, delivery and returns.

A supplement is a complementary feed, not a medicine. It is intended to support normal health and nutrition, not to treat, cure or prevent disease. If your pet has a diagnosed condition or is already on medication, a supplement is not a replacement — talk to your vet first, because some ingredients interact with medicines or aren't right for very young, pregnant or unwell animals.

Paying for a prescription medicine too?

If your pet is on a lifelong medicine — for arthritis, heart disease, thyroid or kidney conditions — a supplement is only part of the picture. You have a legal right to a written prescription and can buy the medicine from any VMD-registered pharmacy you choose, often for far less than the practice price.

Supplement FAQs

Do pet supplements need a prescription in the UK?

No. These are complementary feed, not veterinary medicines, so they don't need a prescription. They are not a substitute for a medicine your vet has prescribed — if your pet is unwell or on medication, speak to your vet before adding a supplement.

How does Maggie Search make money from supplements?

Some links to pharmacies earn us a commission through affiliate networks including Awin. It never changes what we show you, it never costs you more, and the price you pay is the pharmacy's price. The tool is free to pet owners.

Are these supplements safe for my dog or cat?

Complementary feeds sold in the UK are generally well tolerated, but suitability depends on your pet's weight, age and any medication it takes. Read the label, follow the feeding guide, and check with your vet before starting a new supplement — especially for pregnant, very young or unwell animals.

What's the difference between a supplement and a medicine?

A veterinary medicine is authorised by the VMD to treat or prevent a specific condition and often needs a prescription. A supplement is a complementary feed intended to support normal health and nutrition, with no medicinal claims. We compare both but keep them clearly separated.

Who handles delivery and returns?

Maggie Search doesn't sell or ship anything. When you choose a product you're taken to a registered UK pharmacy such as VioVet, and delivery, payment and returns are handled entirely by that retailer under their own terms.
